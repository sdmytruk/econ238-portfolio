What Does Your Dinner Cost the Climate?

A plant-based diet changes more than what is on the plate. It changes demand for land, feed, livestock production and the greenhouse gases associated with producing food.

The climate story is also a land story.

Producing 100 grams of protein from beef cattle uses about 163.6 m²-years of land on the global average. Tofu uses about 2.2 m²-years. Land has an opportunity cost: land used for livestock and feed cannot simultaneously be used for other agriculture, habitat, forests, housing or other purposes.

Where does economics enter?

A diet is a consumer choice, but millions of choices become market demand. That demand affects which products are valuable to produce and which scarce resources are pulled into production.

Consumer demand
→ Food production
→ Land + feed + energy
→ Emissions + resource tradeoffs

Grocery price is only one economic measure. Food production also uses scarce inputs, and environmental damages can be external costs not fully reflected in the price paid at checkout. Retail prices also change over time: USDA's Food Price Outlook, for example, forecast beef and veal prices to rise 9.8% in 2026 amid tight cattle supplies.

The biggest change does not necessarily require an all-or-nothing diet. Which food gets replaced matters.
