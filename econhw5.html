100% Renewable
for One Real Week

Suppose we build enough wind and solar to equal an entire week's electricity use. Does that mean we have enough electricity for every hour of that week?

A 168-hour thought experiment using a real grid week. The finished analysis will use NYISO hourly load and fuel-mix data; the shape below is an intentionally labeled visual model of the calculation.

One week. 168 hours.

The entire exhibit begins with subtraction.

Gapt = Wind + Solar − Demandt

A positive gap is electricity available to store or curtail. A negative gap is electricity the system still needs. Repeat that calculation 168 times and the abstract phrase “100% renewable” becomes a physical balancing problem.

SAMPLE CALCULATION — ILLUSTRATIVE

Demand at 8 PM       = 28,000 MW
Wind + solar         = 19,500 MW
────────────────────────────
Hourly gap           = −8,500 MW

For one hour:
8,500 MW × 1 h = 8.5 GWh short

This example shows the arithmetic only; these are not observed NYISO values.
“Enough renewable energy” and “enough electricity at every hour” are two different questions.
